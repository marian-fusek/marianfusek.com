↓The work

Most of us spend years learning how to perform. Coaching is time spent learning what is actually true for you. I ask the questions you might rather avoid, until your own answer gets harder to ignore.

What it is

  • Partnership: A shared process to explore what matters and decide what comes next, with shared intent.
  • Fresh perspective: Questions that challenge familiar thinking and reveal new options you hadn’t seen.
  • Your own progress: Build on your strengths to move toward goals that matter to you in everyday life.

What it isn’t

  • Not mentoring: I won’t prescribe a path based on my experience; we work from yours, with you leading.
  • Not consulting: I won’t hand you a ready-made fix; we clarify your next step together in each conversation.
  • Not therapy: Coaching looks ahead and supports action; it isn’t mental-health treatment or formal diagnosis.

For people who make, lead and carry the work.

Designers, creative leads, founders, managers and executives often arrive with a work problem that is also a life problem. A career move changes your identity. A leadership role follows you home. A plan for what comes next needs to be yours.

You might be dealing with

  • A career move that feels unclear
  • Work that has lost its shape
  • Burnout, self-doubt or low energy
  • A role that grew faster than you did
  • Pressure to prove yourself

We can work through

  • A decision you keep postponing
  • A conversation you need to have
  • The confidence to be more visible
  • A plan for what comes next
  • What “enough” looks like on your terms

The real you begins where the performance ends.

I listen closely, ask the question beneath the question, and help you separate what you actually want from the expectations you have absorbed. Sometimes that means making a difficult thing clearer. Sometimes it means a push out of a comfortable story. Sometimes it means finally giving yourself permission to stop.

You will not leave with somebody else’s life plan. You will leave with a clearer relationship to yourself, your choices and the next thing that is yours to do.

Things I ask.

  1. If nobody was impressed by your next move, what would you choose?
  2. What are you protecting by staying in a situation you keep saying you want to leave?
  3. What do you want that you’ve trained yourself to dismiss?
  4. Whose approval are you still chasing?
  5. What are you refusing to admit?
